Data Science Experience

For the graduate capstone project, I collaborated with another student and built the risk-classification workflow and topic–risk integration components of an NLP decision-support pipeline for Reddit mental health posts. I used approximately 1,200 annotated posts out of a cleaned corpus of 87,733 posts to train and compare a TF-IDF and logistic-regression baseline with DistilBERT, SBERT, and RoBERTa for four-level risk classification and binary high-risk triage. On the held-out test set, RoBERTa offered the best balance among the models evaluated, achieving 0.846 high-risk recall, 0.800 precision, and 0.867 PR-AUC. I then combined its predictions with my teammate's BERTopic results for 34,146 posts to visualize how discussion themes relate to predicted risk levels using a heatmap. The analysis highlighted elevated risk in topics containing explicit self-harm language and showed how the pipeline could help moderators focus their review. Throughout the project, we treated model outputs as triage signals requiring human judgment and documented limitations in label quality, generalizability, bias, and privacy. Please view the poster and find the GitHub repository that contains the code.

This is a project for a Data Mining course. The objective of this project was to understand the relationship between various types of cancer and Area Deprivation Index (ADI) in Metropolitan Statistical Areas (MSA). I used multiple datasets - a cancer dataset, an ADI dataset, and two geospatial datasets to perform analyses and analyze the relationships between cancer types and ADI in MSA. The preprocessing involved data cleaning in order to remove the non-relevant information, change data types, and drop NaN values. Then, I performed geospatial joins in order to join the cancer and ADI datasets on census block groups. The methods I used include dimensionality reduction using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), correlation analysis, cluster analysis using KMeans clustering, and topic modeling using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). Lastly, I created a presentation for the project.

This is a final course project for a Networks course. I collaborated with three other students to investigate how actor and crew success in the film industry is determined based on their collaboration patterns within the industry. This project utilized the TMDB 5000 Movie Dataset from Kaggle that allowed the team to incorporate rich information on cast, crew, genres, revenues, and budgets. The methods used were Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), Community Detection, and Centrality Measures (such as eigenvector centrality and degree centrality). The final presentation incorporates research questions, motivation, methods, and results.
